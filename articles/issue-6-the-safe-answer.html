The Safe Answer

Published June 12, 2026 · Brandon Labonte, SHRM-CP

A few years ago, I was helping a candidate think through a career move. There was nothing unusual about her situation. She had a good resume, solid experience, and a stable career. On paper, everything looked fine. In fact, she was a strong fit for the opportunity we were discussing.

When I asked why she was exploring opportunities, she gave me the same answer recruiters hear every day.

"I'd like to make more money." Fair enough, was what I thought.

Had I stopped there, we probably would have spent the next thirty minutes discussing compensation, bonus plans, benefits, and salary ranges. But something about the answer felt incomplete. Not wrong. Just incomplete.

So I stayed curious.

As the conversation continued, we moved beyond compensation and started talking about growth, recognition, learning, work-life balance, and the type of impact she wanted to make through her career. That's when something shifted. She stopped answering my questions and started answering her own.

There was a long pause. Then another.

And then came the tears. Not because of the opportunity. Not because of compensation. Not because she was unhappy with her manager. I firmly believe, She became emotional because she suddenly realized how many things that mattered most to her had been missing for years. The money was real, but it wasn't the story. It was the safe answer.

The deeper answer was that she had stopped growing. She had stopped learning. She no longer felt challenged. Somewhere along the way, she had stopped becoming the person she wanted to become professionally. The conversation didn't create those feelings. The conversation simply brought them into the light.

I've thought about that moment a lot this week because I think most of us have safe answers. We tell ourselves we're just tired. We tell ourselves it's been a busy season. We tell ourselves everything is fine. We tell ourselves it's not that bad. Sometimes those things are true. But sometimes they're placeholders. Temporary explanations, covering deeper realities we haven't fully examined.

The more I reflected on that conversation, the more it reminded me of carrying a rucksack in the Army. I have bad feet and ankle pain to remind me of this lesson.

If someone asked how I was doing, the answer was usually simple. "I'm good."

Meanwhile, my shoulders where numb and tingly. My feet where on fire and hurting. My back was compressed and ready to snap. I was counting every step until the next canteen break.

The facts, The weight hadn't disappeared. I had simply gotten used to carrying it, knowing i needed to keep going. I think a lot of people do the same thing in their careers. And sometimes in life.

They adapt.

They compromise.

They accept.

Eventually the weight becomes normal. Until a conversation causes them to stop and realize how much they've actually been carrying.

I've been reading John Maxwell's The 15 Invaluable Laws of Growth, and one thought keeps staying with me. Growth starts with awareness. You cannot intentionally grow in an area you've refused to honestly identify.

Maybe that's why deeper conversations matter so much. Not because they give us answers. But because they help us hear ourselves. Sometimes the biggest breakthrough in a conversation isn't what someone else says. It's finally hearing what you've been saying all along.

So before you head into the weekend, I'll leave you with one question.

What is something you've been telling yourself is "fine" that deserves a more honest conversation?

Goals are seasonal. Growth is the journey.
