The Hire Is Not the Finish Line

Published May 22, 2026 · Brandon Labonte, SHRM-CP

This week reminded me how much energy organizations put into attracting talent… and how quickly some of that energy disappears after the offer letter gets signed.

Every Monday I help run orientation for our new hires, and if I am being honest, some Mondays are harder than others. There are weeks where recruiting is absolutely slammed. Requisitions piling up, phones ringing, interviews needing scheduled, hiring managers needing updates, candidates needing feedback, and pressure everywhere to keep positions moving. And then Monday morning arrives with onboarding paperwork, technology setup, policies, safety videos, badge photos, training schedules, and all the operational pieces that come with bringing someone new into the company.

Some mornings, I honestly do not feel like doing orientation at all.

Not because I do not care, but because my brain is already racing toward the next staffing problem that needs solved. But I remind myself of something every single Monday, The new hire should not be punished for my workload pressure.

To them, this is not “just another orientation.”

This is their first real impression of the organization. Their first sense of leadership. Their first feeling about the culture. Their first emotional reaction to whether they made the right decision accepting the offer. So even with paperwork, policies, and safety videos, I try to bring energy into the room. Conversation. Humor. Engagement. Something that makes the experience feel human instead of transactional.

Because retention starts with relationship. And relationship has to be reinforced early.

That may be the real 3 R’s after the hire.

Retention. Relationship. Reinforcement.

No employee feels emotionally connected to a company because they signed twelve onboarding documents and got a password reset link on time. Those things matter operationally. But engagement is emotional.

People remember how they were welcomed. They remember whether leadership interacted with them. They remember whether anyone seemed genuinely prepared for their arrival. They remember whether conversations felt intentional or transactional.

I think a lot of organizations focus heavily on attracting talent and unintentionally breeze right past the relationship-building part once the offer letter is signed. That is dangerous in today’s market. Because employees do not emotionally commit to organizations the day they accept the offer.

That part happens through consistency, communication, connection, and reinforcement after they arrive.

The hire is not the finish line. It is the starting line.
