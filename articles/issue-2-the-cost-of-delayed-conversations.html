The Cost of Delayed Conversations

Published May 15, 2026 · Brandon Labonte, SHRM-CP

This week was a reminder that strong candidates rarely stay available for long.

I lost one candidate earlier this week to another offer, and then another one the very next morning. The frustrating part was not losing them. Recruiting in a competitive market comes with losses. The part that stuck with me was realizing both candidates originally had interviews scheduled with us the week before.

Both interviews had to be postponed at the last minute because leadership had operational fires they needed to put out. And to be clear, I understand that.

Business needs are real. Customers need support. Emergencies happen. Leaders in operations and service environments are constantly balancing priorities that can change in an instant.

But delayed conversations can become expensive conversations.

When we rescheduled the interviews, one candidate accepted another opportunity before the interview even happened. The other did make it to the interview, but already had another offer in hand by then.

I asked both of them candidly when those opportunities moved forward. The answer was basically the same from each of them. “We went on another interview after ours got postponed.” Ouch for sure, that part stayed with me all week because candidates interpret urgency too.

Most organizations think candidates are only evaluating compensation, benefits, and job titles. In reality, they are also evaluating responsiveness, consistency, communication, and priority long before an offer is ever discussed. And sometimes one postponed interview is enough to quietly shift momentum somewhere else.

I think this is where recruiting and operational leadership have to stay aligned. Speed alone does not create better hiring outcomes. Deep conversations, understanding, and alignment still matter tremendously. But delayed conversations create openings for another company to build connection first. And sometimes that is all it takes.

The strongest hiring processes I have seen protect two things very carefully, the quality of the conversation and the consistency of the process. Because once momentum breaks, alignment becomes much harder to recover. Candidates do not just remember what was offered.

They remember how the process felt.
